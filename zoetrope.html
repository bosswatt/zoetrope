What r3 adds. A second output: the reel as a GIF made of characters. The GIF is read with REEL r2's parser, each kept frame is laid over a grid of character cells (COLUMNS across; rows follow the picture; a cell is 6 × 8), and each cell's tone picks a character from the chosen ramp. The three ramps are the ASCII RASTERIZER's, character for character; each is re-ordered by the ink its characters really carry in the built-in face, so tone never runs backwards, and a cell leans a little toward its inkiest part so thin strokes do not wash out. The type is then drawn back into pixels with that face — 5 × 7 in a 6 × 8 cell, built into this file, so no font is asked of the device and every device writes the same GIF — and packed by a GIF writer that is also in this file.

Fitted once for the reel, not per frame. Levels: the reel's 0.5 % – 99.5 % tones become the full range. Paper: AUTO sets a mostly light reel dark-on-light and a dark reel light-on-dark, so the ground stays blank and the ink is the picture. Ink: COLOR gives each character the least ink that makes its cell's color on that paper, from one palette of up to 62 inks fitted across the whole reel; MONO, GREEN and AMBER are one ink. INVERT trades light for dark and keeps the hues, so a white-ground diagram becomes the same diagram on black. Nothing pumps or re-tints between frames.

Kept still. A cell keeps last frame's character and ink while they still fit, so flat areas do not shimmer. Each frame after the first is written as only the box of cells that changed, with unchanged cells inside it left transparent; frames that set to identical type become one longer hold. SPEED rewrites the delays and the three single inks rewrite the color table — nothing is set or packed again; SIZE packs the same type at a new pixel size; everything else re-sets the reel, and the scan is kept while the crop stays.

The GIF it writes. GIF89a, one global color table, loops forever. Width and height are whole cells: COLUMNS × 6 and rows × 8, times SIZE. Delays are the source's, in hundredths of a second, never under 20 ms (browsers slow anything faster). COPY FRAME AS TEXT puts the frame on the stage on the clipboard as plain text, one line per row. The stage plays the same cells the file holds — pause, step and scrub work as they do for the SVG.

The SVG it writes — r2, unchanged. Every kept frame is cut with LINEFINDER v3 exactly as CHESHIRE r1 cuts its DETAIL pass: a 224 px working grid (144 · 320 · 448 on offer), the palette swept 10 · 14 · 18 and knee-picked by LINEFINDER's own sanity check, regions down to five ten-thousandths of the frame allowed to live, Douglas-Peucker ε 0.8, no 90° snap; the palette is fitted once across the reel and the sweep is graded on three frames. One <g> per frame, each carrying a single <animate attributeName="opacity" calcMode="discrete"> that turns it on for its slice of the loop; frames after the first start at opacity 0, so a viewer with no SMIL shows frame 1 as a still. Width and height are the crop's pixels; the viewBox is the working grid.

Crop and frames — both outputs. Crop is set on the source, in GIF pixels, and applied at the cut. Identical consecutive frames are merged into one hold, judged inside the crop. Past the FRAMES cap the reel is thinned evenly by index and each kept frame inherits the time of the frames it stands in for, so the loop stays as long as the GIF; ALL keeps every unique frame. Delays under 20 ms read as 100 ms, the way browsers read them. Frames are decoded when they are reached, so a big reel costs its compressed size in memory and little more. The ASCII output re-reads the reel whenever a setting changes, so it unpacks frames with a faster table-driven reader that follows REEL r2's rules step for step.
